About

Siraphop (Mag) Saisa-ard is a Research Associate in the Communications Technology Laboratory at NIST, where he studies how millimeter-wave signals propagate through real environments. His current work spans double-directional channel sounding at 28 GHz, ray-tracing scene calibration with NVIDIA Sionna, and measurement-based contributions to 3GPP RAN1 discussions on ISAC channel blockage modeling.

He holds a Master's degree from the Antenna and Propagation Laboratory at Tokyo Institute of Technology, where his thesis proposed a knife-edge diffraction model for predicting vehicle shadowing in site-specific wireless channel emulators — later validated against full-wave electromagnetic simulation. Between degrees, he spent a year applying machine learning to biomedical signals, working with a hospital in Thailand to design and collect a PPG–EKG arrhythmia dataset.

Thai by birth, he studied and worked in Japan for six years on a MEXT scholarship before joining NIST in Maryland, and speaks English, Thai, and Japanese.
